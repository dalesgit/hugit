I can still so vividly remember the day in 1982, sitting in my office at Trinity Episcopal Church in Wauwatosa, WI. I felt a whirlwind of anxiety swirl around me at the thought that the rest of my life I would be making my living by the words that I spoke (or wrote).

- I thought I am not up to the task
- I hadn't done all that well to date, although some of the awards on paper sounded high fallutin'.
- How will I do it
- Remember, I said to myself, "I think I can." "I think I can." "I think I can."

And so to this day I think that words are central to who I am. Though my wife and much of my reflections through the years have relied on a "via negativa" approach to the deeper things of life. Often I need silence. But the significance of the words is all the greater.

In the beginning was the Word. God saw the light and it was good.

Indeed, but I'm inclined to think that God didn't say them the we ourselves speak. Language preceded writing by tens or hundreds of thousands of years. Many cultures have survived for long periods of time with now written language -- thank you very much.

I think that at the very least God **sang** his creation into existence.
